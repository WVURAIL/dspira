The Wildwood Institute for STEM Research and Development is a student research
lab in Los Angeles. The group built these telescopes and operates them beside a [Quarknet](https://quarknet.org/) cosmic-ray detector. They look for radio bursts coinciding with air showers, extending beyond the DSPIRA curriculum.

They investigate radiation from high-energy particle cascades in the atmosphere, including Cherenkov and bremsstrahlung emission. A cosmic-ray shower and radio transient might therefore be two observations of the same event. Detecting that coincidence is difficult. It remains an open research question, with no predetermined classroom answer.

## What they have published

**[Preparations for the Detection of Radio Waves Created by High-Energy Cosmic
Rays using the WISRD Cosmic Ray Detector](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IJ3-HkYX14fHSujKklYOS62CL6nTMqmP/view)**
— L. Perttula, R. Cortez, S. Zohar, J. Reis and J. A. Wise. The opening paper
of the *WISRD Research &amp; Engineering Journal*, Volume 4 Issue 1, Spring 2022,
pages 2 to 12. The report describes four scintillators, their photomultiplier tubes, and a Fermilab data acquisition board. It explains the detector's calibration and integration with radio telescopes.

**[Cosmic ray detector calibration, spring 2023](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1blapCsGn7M0rHwacW5Ehk3zLXRY6rN84/view)**
— a progress poster. They are replacing a voltage box and stepper motors with Raspberry Pi-controlled digital potentiometers for remote calibration. They also report an unexplained oscillation in anomalous-event rates that remains under investigation.
